# Exploratory models specification (written before any exploratory result existed)

Date: 2026-10-13, after the confirmatory, secondary and robustness results and before any exploratory result. **No exploratory coefficient, interval, p-value, cluster or power figure exists at the time of writing**; nothing in this note was computed on the outcome. It fixes the details that `config/registry/exploratory.toml` and `docs/preregistration.md` section 11 leave open, in the style of `docs/robustness_spec.md` and `docs/secondary_spec.md`. No pre-registered quantity changes. Results are reported whatever they show; later changes need a dated entry in `docs/amendments.md`. Frozen files (`docs/freeze_manifest.json`) stay byte-identical; code is new (`src/models/py/exploratory.py`, `tests/test_exploratory.py`).

**What the author had seen when this was written (disclosure).** The headline M1 estimates (`reports/results/m1_confirmatory.md`), the ten secondary outcomes and the 52 robustness specifications. The choices below come from the registry text, the Gate 2 audit and the data-availability facts in section 1, not from any estimate involving a rule index, a debt split, an EU/euro split or a nonlinear ageing term. The only outcome-derived inputs are the noise calibration of the power block (the same Gate 3b/3c residual calibration disclosed in the pre-registration, section 12) and the E6 features (section 8).

Everything here is exploratory: associations, never causal; scheduled elections are predetermined, not exogenous. The fiscal-rule index is a **de jure design-strength score of national numerical rules** (Spearman 0.83 with rule count in the audit), not enforcement or compliance.

## 2. Common machinery (all of E1-E4)

* **Outcome:** `capb` = AMECO `UBLGBP`, pp of potential GDP, surplus positive, Spring 2026 vintage (UBLGIE and the secondary outcomes are not used). No separate UBLGBP-versus-UBLGIE analysis is run.
* **Estimator:** V7 = `GlsEstimator(design, wls=True, ar1=True)` from `src/evaluate/power_variants.py` (feasible WLS plus AR(1) feasible GLS, Nickell-corrected rho, country and year effects, CR1 by country), exactly as in `m1.py`/`secondary.py` (reused by import, not copied). The designs are built with `power.build_design(frame, label, regressors, targets, scale)` from regressor frames produced by `pv.variant_regressors` plus the added columns of each model. Rows are sorted by country and year; a gap breaks a run (the Prais-Winsten step restarts); a column with no variation is dropped and reported; a rank-deficient design is reported as `failed` with the reason, never re-specified.
* **M1 base terms** (recomputed by `power.regressors_from_frame(frame, "m1")` on each model's own estimation sample, as the secondary spec does): `W_S`, `W_E`, `W_D` (if not constant), `W_U`, `Post`, `A_lag` (lagged OLDDEP1 standardised to mean 0, SD 1, population form), `WS_x_between` (= W_S * abar_i / sd(abar)), `WS_x_within` (= W_S * (z - abar_i) / sd(z - abar)), and, for the full-sample models only, the centred era terms of `pv.era_terms`. Standardisation, the Mundlak split of ageing and the era centring are therefore recomputed on the model's own sample (the rule used for the secondary outcomes).
* **Moderators added in this spec** are standardised or centred as stated per model; every added interaction carries its main effect when the moderator varies within country; a time-invariant main effect is absorbed by the country effects and is not entered.
* **Centred binary moderators** (E4): `D_c = D - m` with `m = sum(W_S * D) / sum(W_S)` (the exposure-weighted mean, the rule that centres the era terms), so `beta_S` remains the association at the average composition of exposure and the added coefficient is the difference between the D = 1 and D = 0 groups.
* **Inference for every target of a family model:** p from `pv.wild_bootstrap_pvalues(est, target, y[:, None], weights="rademacher", n_boot=9999, seed_key=42)` (null imposed by dropping the target column, whole feasible procedure re-estimated in every bootstrap sample); 95% interval from `m1.unrestricted_bootstrap_t` (unrestricted wild bootstrap-t percentile interval, same weights, B = 9,999, seed key 42). Two-sided. CR1 t(G-1) is stored for transparency and labelled not valid for this estimator (as in the headline); **no CR1 p-value is a primary result**. Descriptive companions get the interval only (no p-value).
* **Estimator feasibility rule (E1, E2; the 6-year window).** V7's gain was calibrated at about 26 years per country; at a mean of 5.93 years per country the Nickell correction and the per-country variance weights rest on little data. V7 is attempted first and is the primary estimator **unless** one of these mechanical checks fails on the point-estimate fit: (F1) the design builds (full rank); (F2) the raw standardised pooled AR(1) of the first-stage residuals lies strictly inside the range of the Nickell map at the window's mean number of years (`pv.RHO_GRID`, `power.nickell_bias`; for 5.93 years the map spans plim -0.958 to 0.921), and the corrected rho is below `pv.RHO_CAP` = 0.95; (F3) no country's first-stage residual variance hits the floor in `GlsEstimator._weights` (1e-12 times the maximum); (F4) every bootstrap t statistic is finite. On failure the primary estimator becomes feasible WLS only (V3: `wls=True, ar1=False`), then, if that also fails, two-way OLS (V0: `wls=False, ar1=False`), each with the same bootstrap. The estimator used is named in every output row. In addition V3 and V0 are **always reported beside V7** for E1 and E2 as sensitivity rows, not used to choose.
* **Randomness:** bootstrap seed key 42 for every headline-style test (as in the headline, secondary and robustness runs); leave-one-out fits use seed key 42 with B = 999. Power simulations and k-means starts use `power.make_rng(5000, ...)`; key 5000 is unused elsewhere in `src` (power.py uses 0-5 and 80-99, power_variants.py 3000 with sub-keys).
* **Guard:** the real panel is read only through `m1.load_real_panel()` (tag `preregistration-v1` required, extract hash verified, 715 rows, 27 countries); results go to `reports/` only for real-panel runs.

## 3. E1: fiscal-rule moderation (2016-2021)

* **Moderator:** `F_it` = raw C-FRSI of country i in year t-1, "." kept missing (rows dropped, never 0 or the standardised floor). The standardised sheet is not used (audit). `F_z` = F standardised to mean 0, SD 1 (population form) **on the E1 estimation sample (N = 160)**; "per +1 SD" = 3.759 index points.
* **Sample:** confirmatory rows with year 2016-2021 and non-missing lagged index: **N = 160, 27 countries**. Era terms are not built (`era=False`: the window lies in one era, and a centred era column would be a numerical zero).
* **Regressors:** M1 base terms without era terms (`W_S, W_E, W_U, Post, A_lag, WS_x_between, WS_x_within`) + `F_z` + `WS_x_F` (= W_S * F_z). 9 regressors, 5 year dummies, 27 country effects; 160 observations.
* **Target:** `gamma_F` = coefficient of `WS_x_F`: the change in the W_S association (pp of potential GDP per full year of scheduled-election exposure) per +1 SD of the lagged rule index, at the sample-average ageing moderators. One pooled coefficient, as in the Gate 3b stretch (`power.fiscal_rule_mde`). Enters the family.
* **Companion (descriptive, interval only):** the Mundlak split `WS_x_Fbetween` (W_S * Fbar_i / sd(Fbar)) and `WS_x_Fwithin` (W_S * (F_z - Fbar_i) / sd(F_z - Fbar)) with `Fbar_i` the country mean of `F_z` over the sample, replacing `WS_x_F`; `F_z` stays as the main effect (the same convention as `A_lag` in M1). The between part is cross-country heterogeneity and confounded by country traits; the within part rests on the 30 changes above.
* **Sensitivities (reported, none selected):** (a) V3 and V0 beside V7; (b) **leave-one-country-out**, 27 fits (N = 160 minus the country's rows), V7 or the fallback in force, B = 999: for `gamma_F` the estimate, interval and p of every fit, the range, the country whose removal moves the estimate most, and the number of fits that change the sign or the exclude-zero reading; (c) **without the ageing interactions** (drop `WS_x_between` and `WS_x_within`, keep `A_lag`); (d) **2012-2021 window** (N = 254, lagged index numeric; the 2012-2015 index years are the producer's back-cast zone, flagged): the same model plus the single centred era term `WS_x_era_2016_2021` from `pv.era_terms(frame, breaks=((2016, 2021),))` (both eras would be collinear with W_S once centred); (e) **rule-presence dummy**, on the 2012-2021 window with "." coded 0 (the audit's ambiguous "no rule or no answer" reading, flagged): `P_it = 1` if the lagged index is numeric, `WS_x_P` centred as in section 2, `P_it` as main effect, target `delta_P`; N = 270; identified from CZ, SI, MT, HU, EL only, so it is a coarse check, not a replication; (f) **NL 2021-22**: see below.
* **NL 2021-22 rule (chosen):** treat the two NL index values (raw 12.9158 in 2021 and 2022) as **suspected data error and set them to missing** in the parser (never imputed), with the standardised sheet and the 2023 reversal as the reason (audit question 5 is unanswered by the producer; whether it is an error or a real coverage change cannot be settled from the repository). The requested sensitivity that includes them is **identical by construction**: they would be the lagged moderator of t = 2022 and 2023, outside every sample. The code therefore does not re-estimate; it asserts (and a test checks) that the frames with and without the rule are identical for year <= 2021 and that no NL row uses an index year after 2020. If the sample were extended, NL 2022-2023 rows would be dropped.
* **Not run:** the audit's suggestion to drop post-2015 changes not tied to a rule event (the rule-event matching is itself ambiguous in the audit): not run, recorded as a limitation. 15 of the 42 post-2015 changes in the audit had no rule entry or exit.
* **Reading:** the index is retroactively revised and is the producer's March 2026 knowledge, not what was known in year t; moderation by de jure design is not moderation by enforcement.

## 4. E2: triple interaction (E1 window only)

* **Sample and estimator:** exactly E1 (N = 160, 27 countries, section 2 feasibility rule).
* **Regressors:** E1 pooled model + `B_x_F` (= B_i * F_z, where `B_i = abar_i / sd(abar)` is the standardised between component of ageing, time-invariant, so the product varies with F within country) + `WS_x_B_x_F` (= W_S * B_i * F_z). 11 regressors.
* **Target:** `delta_BF` = coefficient of `WS_x_B_x_F`: the change in the per-SD rule moderation of the W_S association per +1 SD of the between ageing component (and symmetrically). Enters the family. Only the between component of ageing enters the triple term; the within component is not powered even linearly (gamma_W MDE 1.09 pp) and `adev x F` plus `W_S x adev x F` would add two parameters to 160 rows, so they are omitted (stated limitation).
* **Flag:** severely underpowered by design (160 rows, a country-level moderator interacted with a mostly cross-country index). Reported with its interval and the MDE in section 7; read as an estimate of how wide the uncertainty is, not as a test of a hypothesis. No leave-one-out (extra fits add forking paths without information); V3 and V0 rows as in section 2.

## 5. E3: nonlinear ageing (full sample)

* **Sample, estimator, base:** the headline sample and design (N = 715, 27 countries, era terms, V7, M1 base terms of section 2).
* **How the Mundlak split is kept:** M1 interacts W_S with the between component (`B_i = abar_i / sd(abar)`, 27 distinct values) and the within component (`adev`). Nonlinearity is imposed on the **between** component only (the cross-sectional dimension is the only one with dense support and the within component is not powered even linearly); the linear within interaction `WS_x_within` and `A_lag` are kept unchanged.
* **Primary (linear spline, one knot):** knot `kappa` = the median over the 27 countries of `B_i` (computed on the estimation sample, so 13 countries lie above it and 14 at or below), no search over knots. Regressors: `WS_x_between` (slope below the knot, now `gamma_B,low`) + `WS_x_hinge` (= W_S * max(B_i - kappa, 0)). **Target:** `delta_H` = coefficient of `WS_x_hinge` = change in the per-SD slope above the knot relative to below (the null is the linear M1). Enters the family. The null-imposed bootstrap drops the hinge column, which returns the headline M1 design.
* **Slopes (descriptive, interval only):** the same fit reparametrised with `S1 = min(B_i, kappa)` and `S2 = max(B_i - kappa, 0)` (`WS_x_S1`, `WS_x_S2`) gives the slope below and above the knot directly.
* **Companion (descriptive, interval only):** terciles of `B_i` over the 27 countries (9 each, ties broken by country code); two centred step dummies (middle and top versus bottom tercile, centred as in section 2) replace `WS_x_between`; targets `tau_mid`, `tau_top`.
* **Expectation stated in advance:** the hinge is highly collinear with the linear term (correlation 0.68 between the two columns on the 715 rows, a design-only fact) and rests on 27 country-level points, so its interval will be wide (reported with the MDE of section 7).
* **Design check done before writing (placeholder random outcome, no real outcome read):** the E1 (k = 14 parameters), E2 (16), 2012-2021 (single era term), E3 (36), E4a/E4c (36), E4b (N = 688) and E4d (37) designs all build at full rank from the specified columns.

## 6. E4: heterogeneity (full sample; one model per split)

Each model is M1 (N = 715 unless stated, era terms, V7) plus the added terms below; the added interaction coefficient is the target and enters the family. The pooled interaction equals the difference between the two group-specific W_S coefficients (the reparametrisation `W_S*D` and `W_S*(1-D)` replacing `W_S` spans the same column space, and V7 depends on the column space only); the group-specific associations are reported as descriptive companions (interval only) and a test checks that their difference equals the pooled target.

| ID | Moderator D | Added terms | Sample | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E4a | `high_i` = 1 if the country mean of `debt_gdp_lag` over its observed rows is strictly above the median of the 27 country means (13 vs 14 countries) | `WS_x_high` (centred); main effect absorbed | 715 | `delta_debt_between` |
| E4b | `highT_it` = 1 if `debt_gdp_lag_it` is strictly above the median over countries observed in year t (a within-time split; the trend in debt is removed) | `WS_x_highT` (centred) + `highT_it` | 688 (27 rows without lagged debt dropped; M1 terms recomputed on 688) | `delta_debt_within_time` |
| E4c | `acc_i` = 1 if `eu_accession_year` >= 2004 (13 countries), 0 for the EU-14 | `WS_x_acc` (centred); main effect absorbed | 715 | `delta_accession` |
| E4d | `euro_member_it` (gold panel; 1 when year >= euro adoption year) | `WS_x_euro` (centred) + `euro_member_it` | 715 | `delta_euro` |

Notes: the split of debt is on the lagged debt ratio (Eurostat `gov_10dd_edpt1` GD, % of GDP, at t-1); the debt median and the adoption years are inputs from the gold panel and `config/countries.csv` and are fixed here. `euro_member` rests on researcher-typed adoption years not cross-checked against the ECB record (gold panel audit). Euro status moves with era and with the 2008-12 crisis; the coefficient is an association with membership status, not with the single currency. The between/within decomposition of ageing is retained in all four models. A Mundlak split of the euro dummy (`ebar_i`, `edev_it`) is reported as a descriptive companion for E4d only (interval only).

## 7. Multiplicity, power, reporting

* **The exploratory family has seven coefficients:** `gamma_F` (E1), `delta_BF` (E2), `delta_H` (E3), `delta_debt_between` (E4a), `delta_debt_within_time` (E4b), `delta_accession` (E4c), `delta_euro` (E4d). **Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.05 over these seven p-values** (reuse `secondary.benjamini_hochberg`), with Benjamini-Yekutieli adjusted values beside it (`secondary.benjamini_yekutieli`) as the conservative check. The family is fixed here and does not shrink if a model is found underpowered (unlike the confirmatory demotion rule D-C, which defends a decision; no decision rests on this family). Everything else is descriptive and outside the family: the E1 split, E1 sensitivities (including `delta_P`), slopes and terciles of E3, group-specific associations and the E4d split, E6. Each reported p-value is flagged raw (unadjusted) or BH-adjusted. A failed model stays in the family list as failed and m is reduced, as in the secondary run.
* **Reported per family coefficient:** estimate (pp of potential GDP per +1 SD of the moderator, or per group difference), 95% bootstrap-t interval, raw p (null-imposed wild-cluster bootstrap-t, B = 9,999), BH q, BY q, N, countries, years, estimator used, and the MDE of the power block. **Reading:** (i) "BH-rejected" if q <= 0.05; (ii) "interval excludes zero, not BH-rejected" (nominal only); (iii) "interval contains zero"; plus an "uninformative" flag when the interval half-width exceeds 1.0 pp (cannot exclude associations above the project's not-powered line). **No equivalence reading against 0.5 pp**: that SESOI was set for beta_S, not for these coefficients.
* **Power block (outcome-free except the noise calibration).** Noise calibration as Gate 3b/3d: `cal = power.calibrate(pv.build_variant_design(extract, "V0n", era=False))` (country SDs, corrected rho; the check is that `cal.rho_corrected` reproduces the Gate 3b value of about 0.69). For each of E1 and E2 build the real design (real moderator, real exposure, window rows), `var = pv.Variant(spec, design, estimator)`, noise = `power.ArNoise(design, cal.sigma, rho)` for rho = `cal.rho_corrected` and rho = 0 (the block-bootstrap scenario is not run for the window: its residual grid is indexed to 27 years), and compute (a) the CR1 size and size-adjusted MDE by `pv.cr1_from_null_draws` with `pv.FULL.n_null`, (b) the wild-bootstrap MDE by the `run_job` pattern (`pv.effect_grid`, `pv.bootstrap_rate`, `pv.power_curve_mde`), with the estimators V7, V3 and V0, per +1 SD of the target and in raw index points. For E3 and E4 the size-adjusted CR1 MDE (a) is reported as indicative (the CR1 test over-rejects for V7). A design-only guide, `r'r` of the residualised target regressor (`Design.r`) against that of `beta_S` in M1, is reported for transparency; the simulation governs. Prior indication (Gate 3b, placeholder moderator, CR1): about 1.1 pp (median; range 0.73-1.78) per total SD at rho 0.69 and 1.6 pp at rho 0 for the E1 interaction, above the 0.75 pp line for interactions; with the real index and V7 it may differ.
* **Promotion condition for E1 (registry):** E1 may return to secondary only if the power check gives a wild-bootstrap MDE of the pooled interaction, per +1 SD of the index at the estimated persistence, of **at most 1.0 pp** (the project's not-powered line, decision D-C; the G2 interaction line 0.75 pp is reported beside it). The check is a design property and is written to the output before the E1 estimate is read. Even if it passes, promotion is an amendment in `docs/amendments.md` and the E1 result stays labelled post hoc; this note does not promote anything.

## 8. E6: country typology (descriptive only)

* **Status:** descriptive. No inference on cluster membership, no p-values, no use of cluster labels as regressors or in any other model, and the typology is **not causal** (it is a summary of 27 countries on five features, not a regime with a mechanism). It uses the outcome: that is disclosed here and is why the labels are not fed downstream.
* **Features** (country level, over the 715 confirmatory rows, means over observed rows unless stated): (1) mean `debt_gdp_lag` (% of GDP); (2) mean `sec_interest_d41pay` (interest, % of GDP); (3) mean `capb` (pp of potential GDP); (4) interquartile range of `capb` (robust dispersion; numpy linear-interpolation quartiles); (5) mean `olddep1_lag`. Each is z-scored across the 27 countries (population SD), equal weights.
* **Method and fixed k:** k-means (`sklearn.cluster.KMeans`, Lloyd, `init="k-means++"`, `n_init=50`) on the z-scores for k = 2, ..., 6. The rule: choose the k with the highest mean silhouette (`sklearn.metrics.silhouette_score`, Euclidean) among those whose smallest cluster has at least 3 countries (so that a cluster is not one or two outliers); ties go to the smaller k; if no k qualifies, report "no admissible k". The silhouette is read on the Kaufman-Rousseeuw scale (above 0.70 strong; 0.51-0.70 reasonable; 0.26-0.50 weak; at most 0.25 no substantial structure) and the result is labelled accordingly.
* **Stability across seeds:** 100 single-start runs at the chosen k (`n_init=1`, seeds from `power.make_rng(5000, 6, j)`, j = 0..99); reference partition = the lowest-inertia run (ties: lowest j); report the share of runs equal to the reference, the minimum and median adjusted Rand index (`sklearn.metrics.adjusted_rand_score`), and the ARI between the reference partition and a Ward-linkage hierarchical cut at the same k (`scipy.cluster.hierarchy`). No pass/fail threshold is set; the numbers are reported and the text must say if the partition is not reproducible.
* **Reported:** the k table (k, silhouette, smallest cluster, inertia), membership, cluster means in original units, and descriptive cross-tabs of clusters with EU-14 versus accession, euro status in 2021 and the E4a debt split.
