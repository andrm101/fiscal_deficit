# Predictive module specification (written and committed before any predictive result exists)

Date: 2026-10-13, after the confirmatory, secondary, robustness and exploratory results and the S4 appendix, and before any predictive result. **No forecast, forecast error, loss, calibration figure or importance exists at the time of writing**; nothing in this note was computed on the outcome beyond counts of non-missing cells (section 1) and, on election dates only, the agreement check in section 4.3. It fixes the details that `config/registry/predictive.toml` and `docs/preregistration.md` section 11 leave open (GitHub issue 31), in the style of `docs/robustness_spec.md`, `docs/secondary_spec.md` and `docs/exploratory_spec.md`. No pre-registered quantity changes. Results are reported whatever they show, with no selection among specifications; later changes need a dated entry in `docs/amendments.md`. Frozen files (everything in `docs/freeze_manifest.json`: `src/`, `config/`, `scripts/freeze.py`, `docs/preregistration.md`, `requirements.lock`) stay byte-identical, a test checks that; code is new (`src/models/py/predictive.py`, `tests/test_predictive.py`).

The module is **predictive and non-causal** (`causal_language = "forbidden"` in the registry). A feature that improves out-of-sample accuracy is a predictor, not a cause; scheduled elections are predetermined, and nothing here re-interprets the pre-registered association (beta_S) in either direction.

The headline M1 estimates (`reports/results/m1_confirmatory.md`); the ten secondary outcomes, including S1c (`sec_d_capb`, the same quantity as this module's target, regressed on exposure, an association and not a forecast); the 52 robustness specifications, including the 2022 extension, which used `capb` in 2022 as an outcome for 22 countries; the exploratory models E1-E4 and E6 (E6 used country means and the interquartile range of `capb`); the S4 revision statistics (revision SD 0.97 pp, mean absolute revision 0.64 pp, `reports/results/s4_revisions.md`); the unconditional descriptives (`reports/tier1_descriptives.md`) and the residual persistence calibration of the power studies (rho about 0.69, pre-registration section 12). **No out-of-sample forecast, no forecast error, no autocorrelation of the first difference of `capb` and no loss differential has been computed.** Every choice below (target, sets, grids, tests, family) comes from the registry text, the data-availability facts of section 1, the structure of the election calendar and the design of the tests, not from any predictive loss. ## 2. Target

* **Definition.** For country i and origin year T: `y_{i,T} = capb_{i,T+1} - capb_{i,T}`, the next-year change in the cyclically adjusted primary balance (AMECO `UBLGBP`, pp of potential GDP, surplus positive, Spring 2026 vintage), **within country, consecutive years only** (a missing year gives a missing target, never a difference across a gap). It equals the gold-panel column `sec_d_capb` of the **forecast-year row** T + 1 (built by the frozen `panel._capb_delta`, an explicit calendar join); a test recomputes it from the raw CAPB series and asserts equality. Unit: pp of potential GDP.
* **Optional binary event (secondary, descriptive, outside the multiplicity family).** `D_{i,T} = 1` if `y_{i,T} < -0.5` pp, the project's smallest effect size of interest (`config/registry/confirmatory.toml`, `smallest_effect_size_pp = 0.5`). It is run only after the continuous module is complete and may be reported as "not run". Models: L2 logistic regression (`LogisticRegression`, grid C in {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}) and `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (same grid and stopping rule as P1b, log-loss), same feature matrices and inner validation; benchmark = the training-window event rate. Metrics: Brier score, log-loss, ROC AUC, Brier skill score against the benchmark, calibration slope of the logit. Note S4: the sign of the one-year change differs between first outturn and final in 14.8 per cent of country-years, so the event label itself is noisy.

## 3. Sample, origins and the final held-out block

* **Rows.** Origin-panel rows are built from the gold panel of section 1. A row enters if the target and **every B2 column** are observed (complete-case on the union, so all models in a comparison are trained and evaluated on identical rows; nothing is imputed). `in_confirmatory` is not used as a selector (it depends on frozen exposure completeness, which this module does not need), but a test asserts that every used row with forecast year <= 2021 is in the 715-row confirmatory extract and that the 1995-2021 part of the 2023 build reproduces the extract's values of `capb`, `olddep1_lag`, `outgap_lag`, `debt_gdp_lag`, `ig_diff_lag`.
* **Training window at origin T:** all complete rows with forecast year Y' <= T (targets realised by the end of year T), first usable forecast year 1997. Expanding, never rolling-fixed-length.
* **Primary evaluation block P: origins 2008, ..., 2020 (13 origins), forecast years 2009-2021, 351 forecasts.** The registry's "first forecast origin 2008" is read as: the last year of information is 2008 and the first forecast is for 2009. All headline statements, the Diebold-Mariano tests and the Holm family use this block only.
* **Final held-out block H: origins 2021 and 2022, forecast years 2022 and 2023 (54 forecasts). Decision: yes, H is used, but as a post-sample confirmation reported descriptively and separately, never pooled into P and never part of a test.** Reasons. (i) The "exposure completeness" problem of the confirmatory sample (frozen exposure needs elections through the end of the following year and is incomplete for AT, CY, EL, MT, PL in 2022) does not arise here: the predictive political features use only elections already held at the origin plus the statutory schedule (section 4), and the calendar is complete through 2022, so H has no missing feature. The frozen exposure columns for 2022-2023 are not read. (ii) Two forecast years cannot support inference (a DM test on two yearly means has one degree of freedom), so H gets point metrics only (RMSE, MAE, ratio to P0, per-year values). (iii) H is a different regime (energy-price support measures, withdrawal of COVID measures, high inflation), which makes it a useful stress of regime shift, and 2022-2023 are the years closest to the end of the vintage, where potential-output revisions are largest. (iv) `capb` for 2022 entered the robustness extension, so H is not pristine; 2023 is. This is stated, not hidden.
* **Cut.** No forecast year after 2023 is used, although AMECO has outturn values for 2024-2025. Reason: the origin for forecast year 2024 would be 2023, and the election calendar is incomplete after mid-2023, so the known-exposure features would be wrong; the vintage forecast years 2026-2027 never enter (frozen reader). Targets in 2022-2023 are at least two years before the first forecast year of the vintage.
* **Eras (by forecast year), as in M1 where possible.** M1's eras are pre-2008, 2008-2015, 2016-2021. With the first forecast year 2009 the **pre-2008 era has no test rows** (it exists only in training windows) and is reported as "no test rows". Evaluation eras: 2009-2015 (M1's 2008-2015 restricted to forecasts), 2016-2021, and H (2022-2023).

## 4. Feature sets: exact columns, lags, and the information set at the origin

### 4.1 Convention

The **origin** T is the end of calendar year T. A feature is admissible only if it is determined by information dated on or before 31 December T. (Real publication lags are ignored in the other direction too: all data are the final Spring 2026 vintage, section 10.) The gold panel's `_lag` columns are dated t - 1 in their own row t, so the origin-dated value T is read **from the forecast-year row T + 1**; the lag relative to the origin is zero. Level and change of CAPB and `euro_member` are read from row T.

| Name in the origin panel | Definition | Source column and row | Dated |
|---|---|---|---|
| `capb_T` | CAPB level, pp of potential GDP | `capb`, row T | T |
| `dcapb_T` | `capb_T - capb_{T-1}` (consecutive years) | `sec_d_capb`, row T | T |
| `debt_gdp_T` | gross debt, % of GDP | `debt_gdp_lag`, row T + 1 | T |
| `outgap_T` | output gap, % of potential GDP | `outgap_lag`, row T + 1 | T |
| `ig_diff_T` | (i - g)/(1 + g), pp | `ig_diff_lag`, row T + 1 | T |
| `euro_T` | euro-area member in year T (0/1) | `euro_member`, row T | T |
| `olddep1_T`, `olddep3_T`, `medage_T` | old-age dependency ratios (65+ per 100 aged 15-64, two definitions) and median age, 1 January | `olddep1_lag`, `olddep3_lag`, `medage_lag`, row T + 1 | 1 Jan T |
| `Wk_next`, `Wk_now`, `Postk_next`, `Postk_now` | known-at-origin election exposure (section 4.3) | new code from the calendar | <= T |

`euro_T` (status in year T, not in T + 1) is used although adoption dates are usually fixed months ahead, to keep the rule strict. **Year dummies, linear or era trends, and any other function of the calendar year alone are not allowed** (they cannot be forecast and would be year fixed effects in disguise); cross-sectional averages of the features at the origin (common-factor information) are also not included, a stated restriction.

| Set | Model-matrix columns (after the country scheme of 4.4) | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **B0** persistence only | `capb_T_dm`, `capb_T_bar`, `dcapb_T_dm`, `dcapb_T_bar` | 4 |
| **B1** lagged fiscal and macro | B0 + `debt_gdp_T_dm/_bar`, `outgap_T_dm/_bar`, `ig_diff_T_dm/_bar`, `euro_T` | 11 |
| **B2** B1 + political + demographic | B1 + political: `Wk_next`, `Wk_now`, `Postk_next`, `Postk_now`; demographic: `olddep1_T_dm/_bar`, `olddep3_T_dm/_bar`, `medage_T_dm/_bar` | 21 |
| B2p (descriptive) | B1 + the four political columns | 15 |
| B2d (descriptive) | B1 + the six demographic columns | 17 |

B2p and B2d only decompose where a B2 gain comes from; they are outside the Holm family. P0 and P0b use no set.

### 4.3 The election calendar at the origin: what is legitimately known

**Why the frozen exposure columns cannot be used at the forecast year.** Frozen `W_*` for calendar year t is the share of t inside the 12-month pre-windows of elections of a class, so it needs elections through the end of t + 1, and the class (scheduled/early/delayed/unclassified) is assigned from ETAD `timing1`, `timing2`, `reason`, i.e. from how the election turned out. For a forecast of year T + 1 made at T: `W_S`, `W_E`, `W_D`, `W_U` of years T + 1 and T look ahead at elections held after the origin, and `W_S` additionally encodes whether a scheduled election was eventually held on time (an early election moves its window from `W_S` to `W_E`); `W_E` and `W_U` are not known at all (early elections are not announced a year ahead). Frozen values are legitimately known at T only for `W_*` of years <= T - 1 and for `Post` of years <= T (`Post` needs only elections already held). Reusing the frozen forecast-year columns would leak the future and would not be a forecast. **The frozen exposure columns, their `*_gf` variants and the completeness flags are therefore not predictors in any primary set.** (`Post` of year T equals the new `Postk_now`; a test asserts equality as a check.)

**What is known: the statutory schedule.** For origin T and country i let K_T = the elections in the calendar (`in_calendar`, all classes, actual `date_used`) with date <= 31 December T, E the latest of them with date d_E, and L_E the statutory term in years:

* L_E = ETAD `termlength` on the row of E (the term of the parliament elected at E); if missing, the last non-missing `termlength` of the same country among elections dated <= d_E; if none exists (CY, MT), 5.0, the value of `expected_term_years` in the calendar (constant 5.0 for both; a legal term, not a data-driven estimate).
* **Scheduled date of the next election** `S_T = d_E + round(365.25 * L_E)` days. Only the next election after E can have a pre-window touching T + 1 (terms are 3-5 years), so no second scheduled date is needed. If S_T <= 31 December T (election overdue), S_T is kept and contributes only to years where its window overlaps.
* **Pre-window set known at T:** the 12-month windows `[d - 1 year, d)` of all d in K_T (realised history) and `[S_T - 1 year, S_T)`.
* `Wk_next` = share of calendar year T + 1 inside the union of that set (in practice only the S_T window matters for T + 1); `Wk_now` = share of year T inside the union. Both in [0, 1], union shares as in the frozen `_union_year_share` convention (new code; frozen module imported only for the helper if convenient, never edited).
* `Postk_next` = share of year T + 1 inside the union of `[d, d + 1 year)` for d in K_T; `Postk_now` = same for year T.
* Classes are deliberately not used. Early elections, delayed elections and a change in term length after the origin are **not known** and are the irreducible political surprise; an election called early inside T + 1 changes the realised exposure but not the features.

**Why not ETAD `sche_date`.** ETAD records `sche_date` of an election on that (future) election's row, by coders who know the outcome; for elections coded on schedule, `dif_days` is 0 in 95.1 per cent of the 143 cases (99.3 per cent within 31 days), i.e. `sche_date` is essentially the realised date. Using it as the schedule leaks the timing choices of governments within the last months of a term. A descriptive check on election dates only (no fiscal data): for 203 elections with a previous election, `date_used(previous) + termlength(previous)` is within 31 days of ETAD `sche_date` in 71.0 per cent; the misses are mainly fixed-calendar systems and elections after early dissolutions. The strict rule above therefore measures the schedule with error but without look-ahead; the code recomputes and prints this agreement. **Oracle diagnostics (not forecasts, outside the family, labelled as leaking):** `B2o` replaces `Wk_next`/`Wk_now` by the same quantities from ETAD `sche_date` of the next election after E (ex-post record), and `B2f` adds the frozen realised `W_S`, `W_E`, `W_U` of year T + 1. They are run for P1a and P1b only, to show how much look-ahead would inflate accuracy; they must never be cited as evidence of predictive value.

### 4.4 Country heterogeneity and standardisation (leakage-free)

* **Country scheme (CD, primary).** For each continuous time-varying feature x (every column of 4.1 except `euro_T` and the four political columns), the model sees `x_dm = x - xbar_i` and `x_bar = xbar_i`, where `xbar_i` is the mean of x over **country i's rows in the current training window only**. For a test row, `xbar_i` is the same training-window mean (the test row's own value never enters it). `euro_T` and the calendar columns enter raw. A country with no training row at an origin cannot occur (all 27 have rows from the first origin), and a test asserts it. No country dummies are used: the target is already a within-country first difference, and dummies would be the country fixed effects that cannot be tuned on a short window.
* **Pooled scheme (POOL, sensitivity, descriptive).** Raw features, no `_bar`/`_dm` split, reported beside CD for P1a and P1b on B1 and B2 and not used to choose.
* **Standardisation.** After the CD transformation each column is centred and scaled by the mean and population SD of the **training window**, applied unchanged to the test rows; columns with training SD < 1e-12 are dropped and reported. Trees receive the same matrices (scale-free but identical inputs).
* **Refit rule.** Every origin starts from scratch: transformation parameters, country means, hyperparameters (re-selected by the inner validation of section 5), and the final fit. Nothing is carried from an earlier origin, including selected hyperparameters (they are stored per origin).

## 5. Models

| ID | Model | Specification |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Naive persistence (the benchmark of all tests) | forecast `y_hat = 0`, i.e. `capb_{T+1}` = `capb_T` |
| P0b | Last change repeated (second benchmark, descriptive) | `y_hat = dcapb_T` |
| **P1a** | Ridge / elastic net | scikit-learn `Ridge` and `ElasticNet` (`max_iter = 100000`, `tol = 1e-8`, fit intercept, no sample weights). Grid: alpha in {0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000} for `Ridge`, and the same alphas crossed with `l1_ratio` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8} for `ElasticNet` (44 configurations) |
| **P1b** | Gradient-boosted trees | scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor(loss="squared_error", learning_rate=0.05, early_stopping=False, max_bins=255, max_leaf_nodes=None, random_state=42)`; grid: `max_depth` in {2, 3} x `min_samples_leaf` in {20, 40} x `l2_regularization` in {0, 1} (8 configurations); iteration count by the early-stopping rule below, capped at 300 |
| P1c | GAM (optional, descriptive) | installed, so **run** for B1 and B2: statsmodels `GLMGam` (Gaussian), cubic `BSplines` with 4 degrees of freedom on the within-country parts of `capb_T`, `dcapb_T`, `debt_gdp_T`, `outgap_T`, `ig_diff_T` and, in B2, on `Wk_next` and `olddep1_T`; all other columns linear; one common penalty alpha in {1, 10, 100, 1000} chosen by the same inner validation; test values are clipped to the training range of each smooth. A failed fit is reported as failed. Outside the family |

Each P1 model is run on B0, B1 and B2 (P1c on B1 and B2). The P1 models are **never** given year information.

**Inner validation (time-series, inside the training window only).** At origin T the inner validation years are the last five forecast years of the training window, v = T - 4, ..., T. For each v the candidate is fitted on rows with forecast year <= v - 1 (standardisation and country means recomputed on that inner window) and scored on the rows of year v. The inner score is the mean over the five v of the pooled squared error of year v. The configuration with the smallest inner score is chosen; ties go to the more regularised one (larger alpha, then smaller `l1_ratio`, then smaller `max_depth`, then larger `min_samples_leaf`). The grids above are fixed here and are not extended after seeing results.

**Early-stopping rule for P1b.** For every configuration and every inner fold the model is fitted with 300 iterations and the squared error of each fold is evaluated at every iteration with `staged_predict`; the inner score at iteration m is the mean over folds, and the stopping iteration is `m* = argmin_m` of that score over m = 1, ..., 300 (smallest m on ties). The chosen (configuration, m*) refits on the full training window with m* iterations. No validation split of the training window is drawn at random (that would leave time order).

**Seeds.** Master seed 42 through `power.make_rng(6000, purpose, origin, replicate)` (key 6000 is unused elsewhere in `src`); purposes: 1 permutation importance, 2 country-cluster bootstrap, 3 oracle and LOCO bootstraps. The scikit-learn and statsmodels fits are deterministic (no subsampling); `random_state = 42` is set anyway and a test fits twice and asserts identical forecasts.

## 6. Validation protocol

1. **Scheme.** Expanding-window rolling origin, one-step-ahead (horizon h = 1 year). For origin T in {2008, ..., 2020} (block P) and {2021, 2022} (block H): build the origin panel; select the training window (forecast years <= T); fit every model of section 5 from scratch (section 4.4 refit rule); forecast every country's `y_{i,T}` for which the target and B2 columns are observed (all 27 in P and H).
2. **Common evaluation set.** All models, including P0, P0b and the oracle diagnostics, are scored on the same rows (the 351 of P and the 54 of H).
3. **No look-ahead, by construction and by test.** (a) Features of a test row are dated <= T; (b) the training targets are those realised by T; (c) country means, standardisation, hyperparameters and the early-stopping iteration use the training window only; (d) the political features use elections dated <= 31 December T and the statutory term, never the realised class or date of a later election; (e) no function of the calendar year enters. Section 12 lists the poisoned-future tests that enforce (a)-(e).
4. **Stopping the pipeline from forking.** The grids, the sets, the family and the readings in sections 5, 7 and 9 are fixed here. Anything not listed is not run; extra diagnostics are labelled descriptive.

## 7. Metrics, tests and multiplicity

All errors are `e = y - y_hat` in pp of potential GDP. P is the base block for every number unless H is named.

* **Point accuracy (all models, block P and block H separately).** Pooled RMSE and MAE; the ratio RMSE(M)/RMSE(P0) and the MSE skill score `1 - MSE(M)/MSE(P0)`; 95 per cent intervals by the **country-cluster bootstrap** (resample the 27 countries with replacement, all their years together; B = 9,999; `make_rng(6000, 2, ...)`; percentile intervals). With 27 clusters the bootstrap is approximate and ignores cross-country dependence beyond the common years drawn together; the year-level DM below is the dependence-robust test.
* **Calibration (all models with a non-constant forecast).** Pooled over block P, OLS of the realised value on the forecast, `y = a + b * y_hat`: report slope b and intercept a with country-cluster bootstrap 95 per cent intervals. Perfect calibration is b = 1, a = 0. P0 has a constant forecast and no slope ("n/a"). Fixed reading: interval of b entirely below 1 means over-dispersed forecasts (shrinkage needed); entirely above 1 under-dispersed; otherwise compatible with calibration. Slopes in H are reported as a point value only.
* **Error by group (descriptive, with n per cell, no tests).** RMSE and MAE of P0, P1a-B1, P1a-B2, P1b-B1, P1b-B2 (the other cells in the JSON) by: **country** (27 cells of 13 forecasts); **era** (2009-2015, 2016-2021, H; pre-2008: no test rows); **regime**: (i) euro versus non-euro by `euro_T`, (ii) high known election exposure `Wk_next >= 0.5` versus below (a quantity known at the origin), (iii) as an ex-post stratum only, forecast years with realised frozen `W_E > 0` (early election in the year; defined for forecast years <= 2021 only), the one thing no forecast could know. Pre-listed sensitivities of the pooled table: excluding Ireland's forecast years 2010 and 2011 (the Gate 3 outlier and its reversal), and excluding forecast years 2020-2021.
* **Diebold-Mariano against persistence, small-sample corrected.**
  * *Loss:* squared error (consistent with RMSE); absolute error as a descriptive robustness DM, outside the family.
  * *Unit and handling of the overlapping panel (choice and reason).* Cross-sectional errors in one year share common shocks (2009, 2020), so country-years are not independent. For each forecast year t = 2009, ..., 2021 the **equally weighted cross-country mean loss differential** `d_t = (1/27) * sum_i (e_{P0,i,t}^2 - e_{M,i,t}^2)` is formed (positive favours M), giving a time series of n = 13 one-step differentials. The effective sample is the number of years, not 351. A pooled panel DM clustered by year would give the same statistic and is not run separately.
  * *Statistic:* `DM = mean(d) / sqrt(gamma_0 / n)` with `gamma_0 = (1/n) sum (d_t - mean(d))^2` (no autocovariance terms: h = 1), multiplied by the Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold factor `sqrt((n + 1 - 2h + h(h-1)/n) / n) = sqrt((n-1)/n)`, which makes the statistic equal to the paired t statistic `mean(d) / (sd(d)/sqrt(n))` with ddof 1. Compared with **Student t with n - 1 = 12 degrees of freedom** (HLN's recommendation). **Two-sided**, because a model can be worse than persistence; the sign is reported, and a "better than persistence" statement requires a positive `mean(d)`. The lag-1 autocorrelation of d_t is reported (descriptive).
  * *Companion interval:* the country-cluster paired bootstrap of the difference in MSE and of the relative MSE reduction (percentile 95 per cent), reported beside every DM.
* **Incremental value of the political + demographic block.** For each model class (P1a, P1b), B2 versus B1 (same class, same grid, same rows): `d_t = mean_i (e_{B1}^2 - e_{B2}^2)`, the same DM-HLN test and the same companion bootstrap. Descriptive decomposition (outside the family): B2p and B2d versus B1.
* **The pre-listed comparisons and the multiplicity family.** Holm (familywise 0.05, reusing `m1.holm`) over **exactly these six two-sided DM p-values, block P, squared-error loss**:
  1. P1a-B1 vs P0
  2. P1b-B1 vs P0
  3. P1a-B2 vs P0
  4. P1b-B2 vs P0
  5. P1a: B2 vs B1
  6. P1b: B2 vs B1

  Everything else is descriptive and outside the family, reported with raw numbers and no adjustment: P0b, all B0 models, P1c, B2p, B2d, the oracle diagnostics, absolute-loss DMs, group tables, the POOL scheme, LOCO, block H, the binary event. Each reported p-value is flagged raw or Holm-adjusted. The family does not shrink if a model fails (it stays in the list as failed and m is reduced, as in the secondary and exploratory runs).
* **Reading of a family comparison.** "Better than persistence" (comparisons 1-4) if the Holm-adjusted p <= 0.05 and the DM numerator is positive; "worse" if adjusted p <= 0.05 and negative; "not distinguishable" otherwise. For 5-6 see section 9. No equivalence reading against the 0.5 pp SESOI (that threshold concerns an association, not a loss).

## 8. Leave-one-country-out stress

* **Question.** Do the forecasts transfer to a country the model has never learned from?
* **Protocol.** For each country c (27) and each origin T of block P, run the same expanding-window pipeline for P1a and P1b on B1 and B2 with **all rows of country c removed from fitting, standardisation, country means of the other countries, and inner validation**, then forecast the rows of c. Hyperparameters are re-selected inside each fit (no sharing with the main run). Country c's own `_bar` terms (section 4.4) are the means of its feature history dated <= T, which is known at the origin and uses none of its targets. Time order is unchanged: training rows of other countries stop at forecast year T. Cost: 27 x 13 x 4 fits (parallelised with `joblib`).
* **Reported (descriptive, outside the family).** Pooled RMSE and MAE of LOCO forecasts and their ratio to P0 and to the same model in the main run; per-country LOCO RMSE and its ratio to the main run; the share of countries for which LOCO RMSE is below P0; the countries with the largest deterioration; and the year-mean DM of LOCO against P0 as in section 7 (descriptive, unadjusted). A model that beats P0 in the main run but not in LOCO is read as relying on country-specific history.

## 9. Interpretation (predictive only, never causal)

* **Tools.** SHAP is not run (not installed). Used instead, all out of sample and for interpretation of predictions only:
  1. **Grouped permutation importance** for P1a-B2 and P1b-B2: at each origin of block P, with that origin's fitted model, the columns of a group are permuted **jointly across the 27 test rows of the origin** (whole rows of the group, so the group's internal dependence is kept), R = 200 repetitions with `make_rng(6000, 1, origin, r)`; importance = percentage increase in the squared error summed over the 351 rows relative to the unpermuted forecast, with origin-level values shown and a country-cluster bootstrap 95 per cent interval for the pooled value. Groups: fiscal-macro (all B1 columns), political (four columns), demographic (six columns). Single-feature permutation importance for every B2 column is reported beside it.
  2. **First-order ALE** (accumulated local effects; own numpy implementation: 10 equal-count bins over the training rows of the last primary origin 2020, centred) for P1a-B2 and P1b-B2, pre-listed features `capb_T_dm`, `dcapb_T_dm`, `debt_gdp_T_dm`, `outgap_T_dm`, `Wk_next`, `olddep1_T_dm` (chosen now; no selection by appearance). ALE describes the fitted function, not the world.
* **Fixed reading of the incremental test (comparisons 5-6).** (R1) If the Holm-adjusted DM p <= 0.05 **and** the bootstrap interval of the relative MSE reduction excludes zero, for both P1a and P1b: "the political and demographic block improves out-of-sample prediction of next-year CAPB changes in this sample, in both model classes". (R2) If only one class: "mixed and model-dependent". (R3) Otherwise, **if the block adds nothing**: the only permitted statement is "no evidence that the political and demographic block improves out-of-sample prediction beyond the lagged fiscal-and-macro baseline, in 13 forecast years; the test can only detect a mean loss differential of about 0.6 SD of the yearly differential (50 per cent power) to 0.85 SD (80 per cent power)" (section 11). Not permitted: "elections/ageing do not matter", "no effect", or any statement about beta_S or gamma_B in either direction; a null predictive increment does not contradict the pre-registered association (a predictable small shift is invisible against forecast noise) and a positive one does not confirm it. The decomposition B2p/B2d may be reported to locate a gain but not to rank politics against ageing.
* **If the block does help,** the wording is "adds predictive accuracy" with the size (relative MSE reduction and its interval); permutation importance and ALE are "which inputs the forecast relies on". The oracle diagnostics B2o/B2f are never cited as a result.
* **Forbidden vocabulary** in `reports/results/predictive.md` and `docs/model_card.md` (a test scans for it): "causes", "caused by", "impact of", "effect of", "driven by", "leads to", "due to the election". Language: "predicts", "is associated with", "forecast error", "out-of-sample".

## 10. Limitations to document in the model card

1. **The target and the features are ex-post revised data (pseudo-out-of-sample, not real-time).** All values are the Spring 2026 vintage. CAPB, the output gap and potential GDP are model estimates that the Commission re-estimates with later data; the vintage value of `capb_T` and `outgap_T` embeds information from years after T (filter end-point and revision effects), so the forecasting problem is easier in some respects and harder in others than a real forecaster's. Comparisons across models share this contamination, but the level of accuracy is not an operational forecast skill.
2. **Measurement error in the target.** The revision SD of the CAPB level is 0.97 pp and the mean absolute revision 0.64 pp (S4; 42.1 per cent of country-years above 0.5 pp); the sign of the one-year change differs between first outturn and final in 14.8 per cent. Part of the forecast error is irreducible noise of this kind, and a real forecaster would also lack the final `capb_T`. The real-time panel covers 2015-2021 only (Gate 5), and a real-time predictive replication is not planned.
3. **AMECO forecast years and the cut.** The last two years of the Spring 2026 vintage (2026-2027) are forecasts and never enter. The last forecast year used is 2023 (section 3); 2024-2025 are outturn in the vintage but are excluded because the election calendar is incomplete after mid-2023 and because end-of-vintage potential-output revisions are largest there. H (2022-2023) is the closest block to that edge and is flagged accordingly.
4. **Small T per country and few origins.** 13 forecast years in P (2 in H), 27 countries, 305 training rows at the first origin, 629 at origin 2020 and 683 at origin 2022 (counts of complete rows, section 1); the DM test has 12 degrees of freedom and the cluster bootstrap 27 clusters. Country-level RMSEs rest on 13 forecasts each.
5. **Regime shifts and one-offs.** Entry to the euro area, the 2005 SGP reform, the six-pack (2011) and fiscal compact (2013), the 2008-12 crisis, the 2020-21 general escape clause and the 2022-23 energy support measures change the data-generating process; CAPB one-offs (Ireland 2010) dominate squared-error losses. The model has no year effects by design.
6. **Political features measure the statutory schedule with error.** The strict schedule rule matches ETAD `sche_date` within 31 days in about 71 per cent of cases (section 4.3); early and unscheduled elections are unforecastable by construction. CY and MT use an assumed 5-year term and ParlGov-only dates.
7. **Demographic features are slow-moving and nearly proportional to country identity** (old-age dependency, median age); in the CD scheme most of their information sits in the `_bar` columns, which behave like a country effect estimated on the training window.
8. **Common-factor information is excluded** (no year effects, no cross-sectional means), so the models cannot learn year-specific shocks; the DM test on year means exposes exactly this.
9. **Software.** No SHAP, LightGBM or XGBoost; histogram gradient boosting from scikit-learn and statsmodels `GLMGam` are used, so results are not comparable to published SHAP/LightGBM benchmarks.
10. **Associations only.** The module makes no causal claim; the pre-registered confirmatory result is unaffected.

## 11. Power and reasonableness (descriptive, design-only; no loss was computed)

* **Number of forecast origins.** 13 yearly origins in P (351 forecasts), 2 in H (54). With 27 countries the cross-section is large, but common shocks make the year the unit of inference.
* **Width of the DM test.** n = 13 yearly differentials, critical value t(0.975, 12) = 2.179. A two-sided 5 per cent test detects a mean loss differential of 0.59 SD of the yearly differential with 50 per cent power and 0.85 SD with 80 per cent power (noncentral t, 12 df). Yearly squared-loss differentials are heavy-tailed (crisis years), so the realised width is likely wider. The 95 per cent interval of the mean differential has half-width 2.179 x SD(d)/sqrt(13) = 0.60 SD(d).
* **How much can a calendar signal add?** If the pre-registered association (beta_S = -0.379 pp per full year of exposure, headline) were taken at face value and fully predictable from the calendar, the predicted change in CAPB from the schedule is beta_S times the change in exposure; the first difference of the frozen `W_S` has variance 0.160 over forecast years 2009-2021 (design-only, on election dates). The reduction in mean squared error is then at most about 0.379^2 x 0.160 = 0.023 pp^2, well below the SESOI squared (0.25 pp^2) and small against any plausible forecast MSE given the revision noise (limitation 2). **A null result for the political block is therefore the most likely outcome and is uninformative about the pre-registered association.** The same arithmetic does not apply to the demographic block, whose size is unknown.
* The expected persistence of CAPB changes (mean reversion) is not stated here: it would require the outcome, and P1 learns it.

## 12. Outputs and tests (produced by the later phase)

* **Code and tests.** `src/models/py/predictive.py` (new; imports `panel`, `power` for `make_rng`, `m1` for the Holm helper and the tag-guarded real-panel reader; refuses the real panel without the tag `preregistration-v1`); `tests/test_predictive.py` with synthetic checks:
  * **No look-ahead on a constructed panel with a poisoned future:** build a synthetic origin panel, then replace every value dated after origin T (CAPB, controls, demographics, election dates and ETAD `sche_date`/class of elections after T) with extreme values; the forecasts of all models at origin T and the features `Wk_*`, `Postk_*` must be bit-identical. A control test shows that a feature built from the frozen `W_S` of year T + 1 or from `sche_date` of a later row *does* change (the test would catch a leak).
  * Rolling-origin splits: expanding windows, forecast years 2009-2021 and 2022-2023, training years <= T, no overlap of validation years with training years in the inner folds, the first usable year 1997.
  * Target equals the explicit calendar join; a gap year yields a missing target; consecutive-years rule.
  * Schedule rule on hand-made calendars (`S_T = d_E + L_E`, term fallback, overdue case, union shares in [0, 1]); `Postk_now` equals the frozen `Post` of year T on the real calendar.
  * Country means use the training window only (a test changes a test-row value and checks the means are unchanged); standardisation parameters unchanged by the test rows.
  * Diebold-Mariano: on constructed loss differentials with known mean and variance the statistic equals the paired t statistic and the HLN factor; p-value against `scipy.stats.t` with 12 df; sign convention.
  * Calibration slope on a known case (`y = 2 * y_hat + 0.5` gives slope 2 and intercept 0.5; `y = y_hat` gives 1 and 0); metrics (RMSE, MAE, skill) on hand-computed arrays; Holm on a known vector; cluster bootstrap reproducible by seed.
  * The early-stopping rule picks the planted best iteration on a synthetic series; inner validation never uses the validation year for fitting; a fit twice gives identical forecasts.
  * ALE on an additive synthetic function recovers the known shape; permutation importance ranks a planted informative group above a noise group.
  * Frozen-file hashes in `docs/freeze_manifest.json` are unchanged; the rendered report states "predictive", names the family of six, flags raw versus adjusted p, states "SHAP: not run: not installed", and contains none of the forbidden vocabulary of section 9.
