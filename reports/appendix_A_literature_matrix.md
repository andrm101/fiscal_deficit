Key corrections to the rows below: the dependent variable is the **real-time CAPB level** (primary balance minus semi-elasticity × output gap, AMECO/EC spring vintages); the 19 countries are the euro-area members through 2015 entrants, annual, 1995–2019; **an election-date variable and turnout are included, but without timing structure, and their coding is not stated in the PDF**; **age dependency is included as a control (never interacted)**; estimators are panel GLS with AR(1) plus Arellano–Bond; political variables are significant in some level specifications. ### 0.2 Afonso, Alves and Leal (2026): items requested (read from the PDF text)

- **Sample / data:** 27 EU member states, quarterly, Q1:2000-Q4:2024. Macro-fiscal data from Eurostat; election dates and government type from the Representative Democracy Data Archive (REPDEM).
- **Election variable:** dummy = 1 in the election quarter and the three preceding quarters (12-month window); alternative windows (6-month; timing windows). Not annual, and not an election-year dummy.
- **Early vs regular:** "Early elections are defined as those occurring at least two quarters before the expected end of the legislative term" (REPDEM information; following Vergne 2009 and Katsimi and Sarantides 2012). Regular = all others.
- **Outcome:** year-on-year growth rates (and q-on-q in robustness) of revenue and primary-expenditure components (compensation of employees, intermediate consumption, GFCF, other primary expenditure; direct, indirect taxes, other revenue). **CAPB is used only descriptively** (Table 1: election-year mean change in CAPB -0.29 vs +0.07 in non-election years, difference -0.36; Figure 1) and is **not** a regression dependent variable.
- **Estimator:** two-way fixed effects with lagged dependent variables, country-clustered SEs; panel unit-root (ADF-Fisher) checks.
- **Results:** elections associated with higher primary expenditure (about +0.49 pp of GDP in the baseline per the text), no significant revenue effect; **effects only around regular elections, none in early elections**; high-debt countries (>90% debt/GDP) deviate; EU-membership and coalition-government moderation. No population-ageing variable was found in a keyword scan of the extracted text.

## 3. Gap statement

1. Elections and headline deficits/discretionary policy in EU/euro-area samples are documented (Mink and de Haan 2006; Buti and van den Noord 2004; Efthyvoulou 2012; Golinelli and Momigliano 2006).
2. Closest prior work using a structural balance and election timing in the EU is Bednar (2019; full text read): 28 EU countries, 1995-2016, system GMM, WEO total structural balance incl. interest, lagged election-year dummy, timing treated as exogenous, no scheduled/early split; an age-structure control enters but is never interacted.
3. Afonso, Alves and Leal (2026) distinguish regular from early elections but use quarterly expenditure/revenue components and report CAPB only descriptively; no demographic moderator was found in the text.
4. Carnazza, Liberati and Sacchi (2025; full text read) test whether political variables change CAPB pro-cyclicality in 19 euro-area countries; an election-date variable and an age-dependency control enter separately, never interacted, with no scheduled/early split.
5. An ageing x electoral-cycle study of fiscal balance (CAPB or otherwise) was **not found in this search**; ageing studies found concern pension/social spending levels, preferences or voting (Tepe and Vanhuysse; Busemeyer et al.; Vlandas; Haelg et al.).
6. A CAPB outcome with a scheduled-election indicator, treating early elections as endogenous (Kayser; Strom and Swindle), and an age-structure interaction in EU-27 was **not found in this search**.
7. Fiscal-rule moderation of PBCs is documented globally (Gootjes et al. 2021); an EU-27 CAPB x election x ageing x rule-strength design was not found.
8. The search used Crossref, OpenAlex, RePEc/IDEAS and web search; it is not a systematic review, so "not found" is not "does not exist".
