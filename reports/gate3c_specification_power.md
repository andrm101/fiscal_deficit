**Reading.**

* The registry baseline V0 (era terms) has beta_S wild-bootstrap MDE 0.70 pp at the estimated rho, the same as the Gate 3b M1 without era terms (V0n 0.70); the engine reproduces Gate 3b (section 2). The era terms leave beta_S alone but raise the gamma_W MDE from 0.90 to 1.93 pp: with the registry's three eras, gamma_W is 'not powered' in every OLS variant.
* Gains come from the estimator, not from the extra regressors. Feasible WLS (V3) lowers the beta_S MDE to 0.59 pp (16% below V0) and AR(1) feasible GLS (V4) to 0.58 pp (17%), both with bootstrap size 4.4-5.6 per cent and bias under 1 per cent. Controls (V1) and the Ireland indicator or winsorising (V2) change the beta_S MDE by +1%, -1% and -3% on the common DGP; V2i looks better (0.61 pp) only when the noise is recalibrated without the Ireland outlier, which is the DGP change, not the estimator.
* The combination V6 (V0 + controls, WLS, AR(1) GLS) reaches 0.53 pp, 24% below V0, and meets the validity screens (size, bias). Against the G2 line (0.50 pp at the estimated rho, 0.75 pp at rho = 0) it is AMBER under the bootstrap and 'GO' only under CR1, which is not a valid basis here: CR1 over-rejects for the feasible-GLS variants (about 8 per cent for beta_S) because it ignores that the weights and rho are estimated, whereas the bootstrap, which re-estimates them in every resample, has size 5.0-5.6 per cent. The headline is therefore the bootstrap column.
* The AR(1) part helps only through persistence: V4 at rho = 0 gives 0.98 pp, no better than V0 (0.97); the gain therefore depends on the estimated rho of 0.69 being right (Gate 3b caveat 4). The WLS part is robust to rho (V3 at rho = 0: 0.81 pp).
* Demotion rule D-C under V6: beta_S powered (0.53 pp), gamma_B powered (0.65 pp), gamma_W not powered (1.08 pp).
* V5 (lagged outcome) is not recommended: it is the least efficient estimator that still has valid size (beta_S MDE 0.72 pp) and its W_S coefficient is biased -4.1% at a 0.5 pp static effect; its lag coefficient shows the Nickell bias (section 5).

## 1. What was compared and how the choice is made

| id | variant | estimator | DGP(s) run | designated DGP |

| V3 | V0, feasible WLS (1/sigma_i^2 re-estimated per draw) | gls + WLS | F | F |
| V4 | V0, AR(1) feasible GLS (Prais-Winsten, Nickell-corrected rho per draw) | gls + AR(1) | F | F |
| V5 | V0 + lagged dependent variable (bias check) | ldv | F | F |
| V6 | V0 + controls, WLS, AR(1) GLS | gls + WLS + AR(1) | F, O | O |

**DGPs.** `F`: the Gate 3b noise (country SDs and rho = 0.69 from the V0n residuals, simulation-corrected; the block bootstrap resamples its residual grid), used for every variant. For variants that add regressors (V1, V2i, V6) the designated DGP is `O`: noise recalibrated from the variant's OWN OLS residuals. **This changes the data-generating process**, because a model with controls or an Ireland indicator leaves less unexplained variance, and a fair power calculation for that model uses the noise it would face; the price is that the gain is credited only if the added terms belong in the true model. `S`: the V2i noise plus the Ireland-2010 shock re-inserted with random sign and the size of the observed V0 residual at that cell, because a Gaussian country SD cannot represent a one-off outlier; it is the faithful DGP for judging the Ireland treatments (V0, V2i, V2w).

**Feasible GLS honesty.** V3 and V4 (and V2w, V6) estimate the first-stage residuals, `sigma_i^2`, `rho` and the winsor limits inside every simulated draw and again inside every bootstrap sample; nothing is taken from the real data. Adding a true effect shifts these estimators' coefficient by exactly that effect and leaves the CR1 SE unchanged, so CR1 size and MDE use one set of null draws; V5 (lagged outcome as regressor) lacks this property and is simulated directly.

**Selection rule (pre-specified in the task, applied mechanically).** Among variants whose wild-bootstrap beta_S size is within 3.5-6.5 per cent at both AR(1) scenarios (rho_hat and rho = 0) and whose absolute relative bias of beta_S at a known true effect of 0.5 pp is below 10 per cent, choose the smallest beta_S wild-bootstrap MDE at rho_hat; candidates within 5 per cent of the minimum are tied and the simpler variant wins (order V0 < V1 < V2i < V2w < V3 < V4 < V5 < V6). V0n is a reference row and is not eligible.

**V6 rule.** V6 = V0 + the controls that survived T4 + every stand-alone component (Ireland treatment, WLS, AR(1) GLS) whose beta_S wild-bootstrap MDE on the common DGP `F` is at least 5 per cent below V0's with valid size and bias below 10 per cent; only the better Ireland treatment enters; V5 never combines. Composition here: V0 + controls, WLS, AR(1) GLS.

## 9. Limitations

1. **Choosing a variant by power on the same panel is a researcher degree of freedom.** It is mitigated, not removed: the choice uses only noise calibrations and simulated draws (no effect estimate was computed, printed or logged), the rule and the candidate set are fixed before the results, and the registry is frozen only after the choice. It still tunes the specification to this panel's noise structure; the freeze must record the chosen variant and this report's hash.
2. **The DGP drives the answer.** The designated convention credits variance reduction to variants that add regressors (`O`); on the common DGP `F` they do not gain. Neither is the truth: the truth about Ireland 2010 is a one-off shock, which `S` represents with an assumed sign symmetry and magnitude equal to a V0 residual.
3. **Gaussian innovations, a common AR(1) coefficient, independence across countries** (AR(1) scenarios); the block bootstrap carries cross-country structure but understates persistence (Gate 3b). The analytic Nickell correction inside V4 under-corrects rho in this geometry (Gate 3b: 0.56 raw, 0.63 analytic, 0.69 simulated), which costs a little efficiency, not validity.
4. **V1 changes the sample** (complete cases of the surviving controls) and the lag structure of V5 loses the first year of every run; MDEs of variants with different N are not like-for-like.
5. **Bootstrap Monte Carlo error.** MDEs come from a probit fit through simulated rejection rates on a grid of true effects (common random numbers); the parametric-bootstrap SE of the fitted MDE is stored in the job files (`wcb_mde_se`) and is typically 0.01-0.03 pp. Differences under about 5 per cent are within noise, which is why ties go to the simpler variant.
6. **V5** is a bias check: its W_S coefficient is a short-run effect and, in a static DGP with AR(1) errors, it is not centred on the true static effect; the bias column is the finding.
7. **Webb weights** were not re-run; Gate 3b found Rademacher and Webb indistinguishable with 27 clusters.
8. Power is for single coefficients at 5 per cent; the Holm family and any multiplicity adjustment are outside this comparison.
