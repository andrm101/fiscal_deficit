## 3. Control definitions

See the module docstring of `src/features/controls.py`. In short: `outgap_lag` = AMECO AVGDGP at t-1 (forecast years excluded); `debt_gdp_lag` = GD % of GDP at t-1; `ig_diff_lag` = (i-g)/(1+g) at t-1 in percentage points with i_t = D41PAY_t / GD_{t-1} and g_t = GDP_t / GDP_{t-1} - 1, which needs D41PAY_{t-1}, GD_{t-2}, GDP_{t-1}, GDP_{t-2}; `euro_member` = 1 when year >= euro adoption year.

## 5. Why the dropped controls fail, and what it implies

* `ig_diff_lag` is missing in 51 of 715 rows (7.1%); it needs `GD_{t-2}`, `GDP_{t-2}` and `D41PAY_{t-1}`. Eurostat's EDP debt series starts in 1995, so the control cannot exist for 1995 and 1996 in any country (and Denmark's debt starts in 2000). Missing rows by year: 1995: 22, 1996: 23, 1997: 2, 1998: 1, 1999: 1, 2000: 1, 2001: 1.
* `debt_gdp_lag` is missing in 27 of 715 rows (3.8%); it needs `GD_{t-1}`; for t = 1995 that is 1994, which Eurostat does not publish (all 1995 rows are missing), and Denmark's series starts in 2000. Missing rows by year: 1995: 22, 1996: 1, 1997: 1, 1998: 1, 1999: 1, 2000: 1.
* `outgap_lag` is missing in 12 rows: AMECO's AVGDGP starts in 1995 for several new members and in 1996-2001 for others, so the lag exists only from the second year. It is always kept, so the model sample shrinks.

**Consequences (not acted on; decisions for the registry freeze).** (1) The T4 rule as written drops debt on a margin of 0.8 points; a debt series reaching back to 1994 (not in the acquired files) would have kept it. (2) Keeping `outgap_lag` makes the model sample N = 703, not 715, which conflicts with the contract line 'exactly 715 rows'; either the contract is amended for the controls model or the 715-row extract is kept and the control model is reported on the complete-case subset. (3) Euro membership has full coverage (status convention: member in year t when t >= adoption year, from `config/countries.csv`).
