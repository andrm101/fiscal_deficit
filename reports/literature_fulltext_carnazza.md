## 2. Dependent variable and output gap

- **Construct (FV, pp. 4, 8-9, Eq. 6 and 8):** the **level** of the cyclically adjusted **primary** balance, in % of (potential) GDP: CAPB = primary balance minus (semi-elasticity x output gap). Interest payments are excluded; one-off and temporary measures are **included** (they count as discretionary), with a stated robustness run excluding them "available on request" (fn 15, p. 8). It is not the change in CAPB, not cyclically adjusted tax revenue, and not an explicit list of discretionary measures. (The concluding remarks on p. 22 loosely speak of "changes in" the CAPB, which is inconsistent with the level specification in Eq. 6.)
- **Output gap vintage (FV, pp. 5, 9):** the key novelty. OGmatched is the output gap as estimated in the European Commission forecast round at the time of the fiscal decision, taken from a country-by-vintage matrix (the "diagonal"; Italy example in online Table A1, UNRES). Source: AMECO (Figs 1-2 notes, pp. 6, 10). Spring forecasts are used; autumn estimates are said to be similar and robust (fn 14, p. 5, "on request"). **Years 1995-2002 are all taken from the 2003 spring forecast** because the Commission began publishing the variable then (fn 14, p. 5), so the pre-2003 part is not genuinely real time.
- **Ex post comparator:** OG2022 (spring 2022 vintage) and CAPB2022 (FV, pp. 5, 9). CAPBmatched is built from OGmatched; the regressions use only the matched pair (p. 16).
- **Semi-elasticities (FV, pp. 5, 8-9):** EC semi-elasticities (Mourre et al. 2013/2019) held **constant** by country (online Table A2, UNRES); Fig. 2 note says 2019 semi-elasticity. A time-varying version is said not to change results materially (p. 8). Output gap to potential GDP is the EC production-function gap (NAWRU critique in fns 7, 13).
- Primary balance source: not stated in the main text (online Table A3, UNRES).

## 3. Political variables (FV, pp. 13-14, 21-22; sources CPDS Armingeon et al. 2021 and DPI Cesi et al. 2021)

Four groups, all entered in levels in the CAPB equation; definitions of each in online Table A4 (UNRES), so the list below is from the main text.

| Group | Variables and definition as given in the PDF |
|---|---|
| Government | gov_party (Schmidt index 1 to 5, right-hegemony to left-hegemony; ideology of cabinet); gov_type (1 single-party majority to 7 technocratic; longest-serving government in the year); gov_chan (number of government changes per year; the paper's stability proxy); yrsoffc (years in office, appears in Table 4 only); allhouse (government controls all law-making houses); gov1seat, opp1seat, gov1age, numopp, oppvote (size and vote share of largest government and opposition parties; gov1age is in Table 4 but not defined in the text) |
| Elections | **elect** (date of the lower-house election; the later date if two in a year); **vturn** (turnout). |
| Party system | rae_ele, rae_leg (Rae fractionalisation, electoral and legislative, 0 to 1) |
| Institutions | checks (DPI checks and balances, 1 upwards); fed, bic, pres, prop (federalism, bicameralism, executive-legislative relation, representation, after Lijphart) |

- **Election timing:** only `elect` and `vturn` enter. The text motivates them by the electoral-cycle literature (consolidation early, expansion late in the cycle; p. 13). **How `elect` is coded numerically (a date, an election-year dummy, a time-since-election counter) is not stated in the PDF (UNRES; online Table A4).** There is no scheduled-versus-early distinction, no pre-election or post-election windows, and no leads or lags. FV for these absences across pp. 13-22.
- Ideology: yes (gov_party). Fragmentation: yes (rae_ele, rae_leg). Majority/coalition: gov_type, allhouse, seat shares. Government stability: gov_chan, yrsoffc.

## 4. Ageing and demography

- **Yes, as a control (FV, p. 12; Table 3, p. 17).** "Age dependency" = (population under 15 + population aged **75+**) / population aged 15-74, motivated by demographics calling for discretionary action. It is part of vector Z with openness, terms of trade, interest rate, unemployment rate and inflation. Estimates (checked on the rendered table): -0.113*** [0.037] (col 4), -0.110*** (col 5), -0.096** (col 6), -0.093*** [0.036] (col 7), all GLS; **-0.019 [0.059] in the AB column (8), insignificant.** The age-dependency variable is never interacted with politics, elections or the output gap.
- The Table 4 and Table 5 notes say the "macroeconomic fundamental variables used in Table 3" are included but not reported; the exact subset is not specified (UNRES).

## 5. Fiscal rules and institutions

- **FRI (FV, pp. 9-12, Eqs 9-10):** from the IMF Fiscal Rules Dataset (Davoodi et al. 2022), following Gootjes et al. (2021). Only budget-balance rules (BBR) and debt rules (DR), each at national and supranational level = four sub-indexes; each sub-index = coverage + legal basis + supporting procedures + enforcement (coverage and legal basis normalised to unity; the last two are dummies); "flexibility" excluded (robustness similar, fn 18). FRI in [0,1] is the normalised equal-weight sum. The regressor is **FRI_DEBT = FRI x debt/GDP, lagged one year** (L.FRI_Debt). Not a rule-strength dummy and not a Fiscal Council index.
- **Results (FV):** L.FRI_Debt is positive and significant throughout (about 0.025 to 0.079, Tables 2-5; baseline 0.046*** [0.008], Table 2 col 1), read as higher debt with stricter rules going with a higher CAPB. **FRI x OG interaction is insignificant:** -0.173 [0.237] (GLS) and 0.336 [0.347] (AB), Table 2 cols 2 and 5, p. 16; also -0.104 [0.230] and 0.288 [0.353] in Table 4. Entering FRI and debt separately makes FRI insignificant (fn 20, p. 12). FRI_DEBT is found to be a-cyclical (same coefficient for positive and negative gaps, fn 25, p. 21).
- Also stated (FV, p. 22): rules are "prominent" and politics adds nothing further once rules are agreed supranationally; this is an interpretation, not a test of rule strength against politics.

## 6. Estimators and specification (FV, p. 4, Eq. 6)

CAPB(i,t) = a + b1 CAPB(i,t-1) + b2 OG(i,t) + b3 FRI_DEBT(i,t-1) + b'Z + f'V + u. The output gap is entered **contemporaneously** (real-time gap, no lag).

- **Pro-cyclicality coefficient:** b2, the response of the CAPB **level** to the same-year real-time output gap; b2 < 0 means pro-cyclical (p. 4). It is a level-on-level relation with a lagged dependent variable (persistence about 0.45-0.60), not an elasticity of discretionary change.
- **Estimators:** GLS with panel-specific AR(1) and heteroskedastic, cross-sectionally uncorrelated errors (country and time effects as marked in each table; in Table 4 GLS columns 1-6 the country-FE row reads No, columns 7-12 Yes, and a Late-entry-EMU dummy throughout); Arellano-Bond difference GMM as robustness (lagged dependent variable endogenous, L.FRI_Debt predetermined, OGmatched exogenous). Table 1 additionally uses FE with robust SEs. Standard errors are in brackets; AB uses the "conventionally derived" variance estimator (p. 4), i.e. not stated as robust or clustered. No country-clustered or Driscoll-Kraay SEs are mentioned.
- **Instruments:** authors explicitly decline an IV approach (p. 14); AB internal instruments only. The Nickell bias is argued to be small for this panel dimension (p. 4).
- **Interactions with politics (FV, p. 22, Table 5):** OG x gov_chan, OG x allhouse, OG x checks (one at a time, then jointly). **No time-varying coefficients** and no regime-switching in this paper (those belong to the authors' separate revenue-side paper).
- **Endogeneity argument (FV, Table 1, pp. 14-16):** with ex post vintages CAPB also "explains" OG (-0.131*, -0.154***, -0.193***); with matched vintages it does not (-0.007 [0.028], -0.006 [0.020], -0.013 [0.027]); this is the authors' justification for treating OGmatched as exogenous.

## 7. Main results (all FV; signs checked on rendered tables)

- **Pro-cyclicality, baseline (Table 2, p. 16):** b2 = -0.223 [0.042]*** GLS (approx. 95% CI -0.305 to -0.141, computed from the reported SE); -0.194 [0.063]*** AB. With FRI x OG: -0.165* [0.089]. With the t+1 forecast of the gap: -0.210 [0.043]*** GLS, -0.247 [0.064]*** AB. Table 3 (macro controls): -0.195 to -0.279 (AB column 8: -0.195 [0.064]). Authors compare with Gootjes and de Haan (2022): -0.188 for 27 EU countries, 2000-2015 (p. 16). Ex post gap gives larger pro-cyclicality: -0.276/-0.281/-0.334 versus matched -0.218/-0.245/-0.277 (Table 1).
- **Macro fundamentals (Table 3, pp. 17, 21):** do not move b2; openness positive, terms of trade positive, inflation negative (GLS and AB), age dependency negative in GLS; interest rate and unemployment insignificant.
- **Politics in levels (Table 4, pp. 18-19, 21):** b2 stays between -0.204 and -0.387 in all 12 columns. Most political variables are insignificant. **gov_chan** (about -0.21 to -0.35; GLS col 5 -0.206* [0.117]; 5% or better in most other columns), **allhouse** (about -0.72 to -0.91; col 5 -0.720*** [0.272]) and **checks** (-0.12 to -0.26; col 5 -0.261*** [0.088]) are significant in several columns and are read as worsening the balance. Authors treat these as not changing the sign of pro-cyclicality.
- **Insignificant in the full specification (col 5):** gov_party -0.034 [0.059]; gov_type 0.002 [0.073]; yrsoffc -0.025 [0.025]; seat and vote variables; **elect -0.055 [0.171]; vturn -0.004 [0.011]**; rae_ele 1.833 [3.078]; rae_leg 2.598 [2.623]; fed, pres, prop, bic.
- **Election variable, group-only specification (col 2, GLS, elections group only):** elect **-0.300** [0.126] (significant at 5%), vturn 0.000 [0.008]. The text never discusses this coefficient. In the AB columns (10-12) elect and vturn are not included (only the variables significant in col 5 are retained).
- **Other group-only results:** rae_leg 3.358** [1.492] in col 3 (party-system group) and pres -0.198** [0.099] in col 4 (institutions group); both vanish in col 5.
- **Politics x output gap (Table 5, pp. 20, 22):** OG x gov_chan insignificant (GLS 0.022 [0.041]; AB 0.003 [0.062]). OG x allhouse -0.254** [0.117] (GLS) and -0.538*** [0.168] (AB); OG x checks 0.075** [0.032] (GLS) and 0.120*** [0.038] (AB). Jointly (col 4, GLS) only OG x checks 0.059* [0.034] survives at 10%; jointly in AB (col 8) OG x allhouse -0.438** and OG x checks 0.097** are significant. Authors read this as unstable and conclude pro-cyclicality is basically unaffected (p. 22). Two printed anomalies in Table 5 col 4: L.FRI_Debt is printed as -0.454, which is inconsistent with all other columns (about 0.05), and the OG main effect is printed 0.050 [0.152]; both look like typesetting or specification oddities.
- **Role of fiscal rules (FV, p. 22):** concluded to be prominent; politics adds nothing "further" once rules are shared supranationally.
- **Precision of the "null":** the abstract's null is about **b2 stability** (the cyclical response), not about politics and the CAPB level; and in the paper's own tables several political variables are significant in levels, and two interactions are significant at 1-5% under AB. Standard errors for the insignificant political terms are mostly large relative to the coefficients (e.g. elect SE 0.171, rae_ele SE 3.078), so these are imprecise zeros, not tight zeros. No power analysis or confidence-interval equivalence test is reported.

## 8. Robustness and stated limitations (FV)

- Robustness: AB versus GLS versus FE (Tables 1-5); forecast-of-gap timing (Table 2 col 3, 6); FRI x OG; crisis dummy 2008-2011 (fn 10, results "on request"); with and without one-offs (fn 15); autumn instead of spring vintages (fn 14); FRI including flexibility (fn 18); FRI and debt entered separately (fn 20); time-varying semi-elasticities (p. 8); estimates on positive and negative gaps separately for FRI_DEBT (fn 25). Several are "available on request", not shown.
- Limitations stated or implied: cannot control for the effective power of checks and balances (p. 21); results differ from Woo (2009) but samples and polarisation measures differ (p. 22); political results "unstable" (p. 22); 2020-2021 excluded (fn 8); data available on request (p. 22). Not addressed: sub-samples or country heterogeneity, early-versus-scheduled elections, clustered SEs, multiple-testing across about 25 political regressors, the splice of pre-2003 gaps, constant semi-elasticities.

## 9. Does the design test an election-year effect on the level or change of the CAPB?

**Partly, and not as a null political-budget-cycle result.**

- The dependent variable is the CAPB **level** and a variable called `elect` (election date) plus `vturn` sit in the level equation (Table 4), so an election-related association with the CAPB is nominally estimated. But (i) the coding of `elect` is not given in the PDF, so no coefficient can be read as an election-year effect (UNRES); (ii) no scheduled/early split, no year-before/election-year/after timing, no leads or lags; (iii) it is one of about 25 political regressors, never the focus, and not tested in AB or in the OG interactions; (iv) the result is mixed: -0.300** [0.126] in the elections-only specification, -0.055 [0.171] once all political variables are added, so it is neither a clean positive nor a clean null; (v) the paper's null claim and conclusions concern the pro-cyclicality coefficient, not an election effect on CAPB. Statistically the only defensible reading is that the paper does not provide evidence for or against our scheduled-election-year hypothesis.
- The paper therefore should be cited as **related evidence on the stability of CAPB pro-cyclicality to political variables and on real-time CAPB construction**, not as a null PBC study.
